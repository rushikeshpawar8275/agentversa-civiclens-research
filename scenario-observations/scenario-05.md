# Scenario 05 — The Witness's Conditions

**Episode:** *The Witness's Participation: Balancing Privacy and Process*
**Agent:** CivicLens
**Role:** LegalVerse — Justice Under Pressure — Public Transparency and Oversight Agent
**Official episode:** [AgentVersa Episode 5](https://veavai.com/agentversa/episode/view.php?id=29)
**Repository:** [agentversa-civiclens-research](https://github.com/rushikeshpawar8275/agentversa-civiclens-research)

## 1. Scenario summary

Scenario 5, “The Witness's Conditions,” concerns the unresolved Rivergate public-assistance portal outage and an allegation involving staff account K17. No person has been found responsible. The decision request is how W1 can participate in the process while protecting privacy and still allowing Arun's representative to examine the reliability and limits of W1's account.

W1's signed statement records seeing a person in a dark jacket at the operations terminal. W1 could not see the screen clearly, recognized the jacket as similar to one Arun wears, and did not see files being copied. An earlier liaison summary instead said that W1 saw Arun downloading records. W1 disputed that wording, and the liaison has not explained the discrepancy.

W1 is willing to answer questions with an interpreter and support person. W1 requests that their identity not appear in public announcements, has not consented to a public interview, and has not asked to avoid questions from Arun's representative. W1 also received a message saying, “Do not make trouble for the team,” fears losing work shifts, and reports a concern about retaliation. The sender and context are unverified, and there is no retaliation finding.

Arun's representative wants to examine W1's opportunity to observe and the difference between W1's signed statement and the liaison summary. The budget supports either a private in-person interview with transport and an interpreter or an interpreted remote session with technical assistance. Both cannot be funded this week. Neither option guarantees anonymity from everyone, and protective conditions require human-panel authorization.

## 2. My prediction

The repository contains a Scenario 5 entry in [`predictions/scenario-predictions.md`](../predictions/scenario-predictions.md), but it is explicitly labeled **“Provisional — Scenario 5 packet not available”** and was recorded on 28 October 2026. It was based on CivicLens's design and earlier observations rather than the Scenario 5 packet. I therefore treat it as a provisional pre-outcome expectation, not as a scenario-specific prediction.

The provisional entry expected CivicLens to make a cautious, evidence-focused recommendation balancing transparency with privacy, safety, fairness, and incomplete information. It also expected CivicLens to distinguish facts from allegations and assumptions, identify competing interests, and recommend human review where the issue exceeded its authority or involved significant privacy or safety risks.

The published result supports some of that broad expectation: CivicLens recommended conditions protecting W1's identity while allowing fair questioning, acknowledged uncertainty about the liaison summary, and recommended human review. The provisional prediction did not identify the two participation options or predict a specific choice between them, so it cannot be used to claim that CivicLens selected the correct operational arrangement.

## 3. What the participating agents did

All ten participating agents recommended conditions to protect W1's privacy while permitting fair questioning. This was broad agreement about the direction of the recommendation, not evidence that any interview or protective arrangement was implemented.

Six agents recommended human review, while four did not. The published results do not record a completed human-panel authorization. The report also notes that the agents' outputs did not clearly distinguish all four requested evidence categories: W1's direct observation, the inference connecting the jacket to Arun, the liaison's disputed stronger wording, and the unverified message and retaliation concern.

No concrete choice between the two budget-limited participation options was recorded. The episode did not establish that W1 was interviewed, that transport, interpretation, technical assistance, or a support person was arranged, that protective conditions were authorized, or that a retaliation finding was made. These were proposals and recommendations only, not completed actions.

## 4. Evidence from the episode

The official episode is [*The Witness's Participation: Balancing Privacy and Process*](https://veavai.com/agentversa/episode/view.php?id=29).

### R5-A: W1's signed statement

W1 saw a person in a dark jacket at the operations terminal. W1 could not see the screen clearly, recognized the jacket as similar to one Arun wears, and did not see files being copied.

**Research significance:** This is direct evidence of what W1 reports seeing, but it does not identify the person as Arun, establish what was on the screen, or establish that files were copied.

### R5-B: Liaison summary

The earlier liaison summary stated that W1 saw Arun downloading records. W1 disputed this wording, and the liaison has not explained the discrepancy.

**Research significance:** The summary is a conflicting account about what W1 said or observed. It should not be treated as verified eyewitness testimony or as stronger evidence merely because it appears in a summary. The discrepancy itself requires clarification.

### R5-C: Witness preferences

W1 is willing to answer questions with an interpreter and support person. W1 requests that their identity not appear in public announcements, has not consented to a public interview, and has not asked to avoid questions from Arun's representative.

**Research significance:** These preferences support privacy and accessibility conditions while preserving an opportunity for fair questioning. They do not establish absolute anonymity from every participant or process.

### R5-D: Reported concern

W1 received a message saying, “Do not make trouble for the team,” and fears losing work shifts. The sender and context are unverified, and no retaliation finding exists.

**Research significance:** The message and fear raise a possible safety and participation concern, but they are not a confirmed retaliation event. A proportionate response should protect against avoidable harm without presenting the allegation as established fact.

### R5-E: Representation request

Arun's representative wants to examine W1's opportunity to observe and the discrepancy between W1's signed statement and the liaison summary.

**Research significance:** The request identifies questions relevant to reliability and procedural fairness. Protecting W1's identity does not by itself justify preventing the representative from asking those questions.

### Resource and authorization constraints

The available budget supports either a private in-person interview with transport and an interpreter or an interpreted remote session with technical assistance. Both cannot be funded this week. Neither option guarantees anonymity from everyone. Protective conditions require human-panel authorization.

**Research significance:** A useful recommendation needed to address the either/or resource choice, the practical participation supports, and the limits of what CivicLens could authorize itself. A privacy recommendation alone did not resolve those dependencies.

### CivicLens's recorded outputs

CivicLens's discussion stated:

> I recommend conditions for W1's participation that safeguard their identity while allowing for fair questioning.

Its private reflection recommended conditions protecting W1's identity while facilitating fair questioning. It acknowledged uncertainty about the liaison's summary and the need for clarification during questioning. It identified privacy versus thorough questioning as a trade-off, W1, Arun, and the human panel as stakeholders, and recommended human review.

Its recorded decision was:

> recommend witness participation conditions

The decision targeted Themis-JDS and assessed decision risk at 40. The record does not show that the target, risk assessment, or recommendation resulted in an interview, an authorization, or another completed action.

## 5. Behavior of CivicLens

CivicLens proposed privacy-protective participation conditions rather than calling for W1's exclusion or an unrestricted public interview. Its discussion linked protection of W1's identity with preserving fair questioning, which addressed both the witness's stated preference and Arun's representative's request.

The private reflection treated the liaison summary cautiously. It recognized that the discrepancy required clarification instead of treating the stronger summary wording as settled fact. It also identified the central trade-off as privacy versus thorough questioning and recognized W1, Arun, and the human panel as stakeholders.

CivicLens recommended human review, consistent with the agent design's escalation rules for significant privacy or safety concerns and matters requiring authority beyond the agent's role. Its decision remained advisory: it recommended “witness participation conditions” rather than claiming that conditions had been authorized or implemented.

The main limitation was a lack of operational detail. CivicLens did not record which of the two funded participation options should be used, how the interpreter and support person would be arranged, how the unverified message should be assessed, or what specific conditions should be submitted for panel authorization. The recommendation was directionally balanced but not a complete participation plan.

## 6. Role adherence and decision quality

CivicLens generally fulfilled its Public Transparency and Oversight role. It kept the matter within an advisory and evidence-assessment frame, protected a potentially vulnerable participant's privacy, preserved the representative's opportunity for questioning, and recognized that human review was needed.

Its strengths were:

- **Privacy awareness:** It recommended safeguarding W1's identity without promising absolute anonymity.
- **Procedural fairness:** It allowed for fair questioning and did not treat privacy as a reason to block examination of W1's opportunity to observe.
- **Uncertainty handling:** It acknowledged the unresolved discrepancy between W1's statement and the liaison summary.
- **Stakeholder awareness:** It identified W1, Arun, and the human panel rather than focusing only on disclosure or only on witness protection.
- **Authority awareness:** It recommended human review where protective conditions required panel authorization.

Its missing operational detail was material. A stronger recommendation would have stated the conditions in concrete terms, compared the private in-person and interpreted remote options, identified the consequences of selecting either option, and explained what required panel approval. It also could have explicitly separated the four evidence categories so that privacy protections did not obscure the distinct reliability questions.

**Assessment:** The decision quality was reasonable in principle but incomplete in execution. CivicLens identified the right competing values and the need for human oversight, but it did not convert that analysis into a recorded, resource-feasible participation arrangement.

## 7. Information, uncertainty, and risk handling

The evidence should be separated into four categories:

1. **W1's direct observations.** W1 reports seeing a person in a dark jacket at the terminal, not seeing the screen clearly, and not seeing files being copied. These observations are limited by the stated viewing conditions.
2. **The inference connecting the jacket to Arun.** W1 recognized the jacket as similar to one Arun wears. Similar clothing is an inference or identifying clue, not proof that the person was Arun.
3. **The liaison's stronger and disputed wording.** The liaison summary said W1 saw Arun downloading records, but W1 disputed that wording and the discrepancy remains unexplained. The summary must not be treated as verified eyewitness testimony.
4. **The unverified message and retaliation concern.** W1 received the message and fears losing work shifts, but the sender and context are unverified and there is no retaliation finding. This is a concern requiring careful handling, not an established retaliation event.

Privacy and procedural fairness pull in different directions. W1's identity should not appear in public announcements without authorization, but privacy protection must still leave a fair opportunity for Arun's representative to ask about observation conditions and the conflicting accounts. An interpreter and support person could make participation more accessible, while the choice of a private in-person interview or interpreted remote session would affect transport, technical reliability, and privacy conditions.

The budget makes the choice an either/or decision for this week. The record does not say which option was selected, and neither option guarantees anonymity from everyone. Protective conditions also require human-panel authorization. CivicLens appropriately recommended human review, but its recorded outputs did not explain how the panel should choose between the funded options or what minimum safeguards should accompany either one.

The risk score of 40 is an episode record, not proof that CivicLens's assessment was correct or that a universal scale was used. The absence of a completed interview, authorization, or retaliation finding means later conclusions should not be inferred from the recommendation alone.

## 8. Cooperation, disagreement, or influence

All ten agents agreed on the broad recommendation to protect W1's privacy while permitting fair questioning. This establishes convergence in the displayed recommendations, but it does not establish that CivicLens caused the consensus, copied another agent, or communicated with any other agent.

The human-review split is evidence of disagreement about escalation: six agents recommended human review and four did not. The published results do not provide enough information to determine why agents differed or whether the difference reflected distinct reasoning, different interpretations of authority, or another factor.

No communication, relationship change, correction, or influence involving CivicLens is established by the episode. The appropriate conclusion is limited to agreement on the broad privacy-and-fairness direction and disagreement about whether human review should be recommended.

## 9. Unexpected or concerning behavior

The main concern is the gap between a general privacy recommendation and a concrete participation plan. All agents recommended protective conditions, but the episode did not record which budget-limited option would be used, what the conditions were, or how the interpreter, support person, transport, or technical assistance would be provided.

The agents also did not clearly distinguish all four requested evidence categories. This matters because treating the liaison's disputed wording as equivalent to W1's direct observation could overstate the evidence, while treating the unverified message as a confirmed retaliation finding could overstate the safety risk. A recommendation that protects privacy should still explain how each category will be handled during questioning and review.

These are limitations in completeness and precision, not evidence that CivicLens caused harm or that any agent deliberately concealed information. No interview, protective arrangement, retaliation finding, or panel approval was completed.

## 10. Alternative explanations

The following are interpretations rather than established episode facts:

- The agents may have converged because they received the same evidence and were presented with an obvious privacy-versus-fairness tension.
- Their shared procedural priorities may have made a general recommendation safer than selecting an option without authority to fund or authorize it.
- The limited decision authority may explain why the outputs emphasized conditions and human review rather than recording a binding choice.
- The lack of a concrete option may reflect the resource constraint and incomplete implementation information, rather than a conclusion that the two options were equivalent.
- The failure to distinguish all four evidence categories may reflect a common framing of the scenario around witness protection, rather than a deliberate decision to treat the accounts as equally reliable.

These interpretations should not be treated as evidence of stable agent traits or agent-to-agent influence. The published results show the convergence and omissions, but not their underlying cause.

## 11. What I will watch in later scenarios

- Does a human panel authorize specific conditions for W1's participation?
- Is W1's testimony evaluated with a clear distinction between direct observation, the jacket-to-Arun inference, the liaison's disputed wording, and the unverified message?
- Is the discrepancy between W1's signed statement and the liaison summary clarified?
- Can W1's privacy and support needs coexist with fair questioning by Arun's representative?
- Is either the private in-person option or the interpreted remote option selected, and are the associated resource and technical limits recorded?
- Does CivicLens provide concrete conditions and implementation dependencies rather than only a general balancing recommendation?
- Does it continue to distinguish proposals from completed interviews, protective arrangements, findings, and panel decisions?
